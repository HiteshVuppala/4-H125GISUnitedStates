What Belongs on the Map?

If it shaped 4-H in your community, it belongs in the record.

  • Camps and fairgrounds
  • Meeting places and Extension offices
  • Leaders and volunteers
  • Early clubs, projects
  • Photographs, artifacts, and records
  • Oral histories and community memories
  • Important accomplishments and local impact
Impact

Why This Matters

Mapping history makes local stories easier to discover and gives future generations a way to learn from the people and communities that came before them.

  • Connects generations
  • Preserves important local history
  • Honors volunteers and community leaders
  • Introduces young people to GIS and digital mapping
  • Turns technology into a service for real communities
Every story adds another piece to the larger picture of 4-H and the communities it has served.

Quality guide

Strong Submissions Include

Good historical entries are specific, supported by evidence, and respectful of the people whose stories are being shared.

  • The five W's: who, what, when, where, and why
  • Photographs or historical documents
  • Newspaper articles, programs, or other published evidence
  • County or club records
  • Awards, letters, and verified memories
  • Permission to share personal photographs or private records
  • Accurate, respectful, community-centered storytelling
Only share materials you are authorized to use. Ask for permission before submitting personal photographs, recordings, names, or private documents.
